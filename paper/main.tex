\begin{abstract}
Generative video foundation models (e.g., Wan~2.1, SkyReels-V2, Sora, Runway Gen-3) synthesize high-fidelity frames, yet their adoption in professional cinematography, autonomous robotics, and physical simulation is fundamentally hindered by \textbf{spatial hallucination}: the inability to enforce deterministic 6-DOF camera trajectories, maintain metric depth consistency, preserve rigid geometry under perspective shifts, and retain character permanence across multi-shot cuts. Commercial platforms obscure underlying model mechanics, impose prohibitive per-second costs, and lack deterministic 3D control rigs.

In this paper, we present \textbf{Aetheris World Engine}, an open-source, sovereign generative cinema and world simulation platform. Aetheris resolves spatial hallucination via a novel \textbf{Spatial Conditioning Bridge} that couples photorealistic 3D geospatial digital twins (Google Photorealistic 3D Tiles and CesiumJS) with open video diffusion transformers. By translating 6-DOF camera spline trajectories into coordinate-invariant 6D Pl\"ucker ray embeddings $\mathcal{L}_{u,v} = (\mathbf{d}_{u,v}, \mathbf{m}_{u,v})$ and synthetic metric depth disparity buffers, Aetheris directly conditions diffusion cross-attention layers on line-of-sight ray geometry. 

Furthermore, we introduce an \textbf{Autonomous Multi-Agent Director Swarm} coordinating screenplay parsing, 3D cinematography, zero-shot vocal acting (VoxCPM), digital avatar performance (Duix-Avatar), and automated 12-stage non-linear timeline assembly (OpenMontage) with $-12\text{ dB}$ dialogue-aware audio ducking. To secure autonomous execution against adversarial attacks, we develop \textbf{AgentShield v2.0}, an enterprise security firewall providing Unicode NFKC normalization, AST command grammar whitelisting, and FIPS~180-4 standard cryptographic SHA-256 chained audit logs.

Finally, we introduce \textbf{WorldGen-Bench}, an open evaluation benchmark measuring Camera Trajectory Error (CTE), Depth Alignment Score (DAS), and Cross-Shot Narrative Fidelity (CSNF). Extensive empirical hardware benchmarks demonstrate that centripetal Catmull-Rom parameterization ($\alpha = 0.5$) achieves $0.273\ \mu\text{s}$ evaluation latency with a $43.4\%$ reduction in acceleration spikes; Pl\"ucker ray field generation reaches $23.96\text{ M rays/sec}$ with exact zero coordinate translation drift ($0.0000 \times 10^0$); the spatial telemetry governor processes $2,927$ live planetary vectors in $0.327\text{ ms}$ average CPU time ($84.2\%$ headroom under 60~FPS); and AgentShield blocks $83.3\%$ of direct adversarial injection attacks at $8.6\ \mu\text{s}$ P50 latency. In trajectory tracking evaluations, Aetheris achieves a $93.9\%$ reduction in camera trajectory error and a $17.19\%$ improvement in metric depth alignment over unconditioned baselines, establishing a deterministic, verifiable foundation for generative cinema and dual-use national spatial simulation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Diffusion Transformers (DiTs) have revolutionized visual media synthesis by scaling generative modeling to billions of parameters, enabling the generation of high-resolution, photorealistic video from open text descriptions~\cite{ho2022video,blattmann2023stable,wan2025open}. However, contemporary generative video models operate predominantly as \textbf{2D appearance hallucination processes}. They model probabilistic distributions of pixel colors across frames but possess no internal representation of 3D Euclidean geometry, projective transformations, metric dimensions, or physical mass.

When prompted to execute cinematic camera moves—such as an orbital tracking arc around an architectural landmark, a vertical crane rise over complex topography, or a dynamic low-altitude drone pass—unconditioned video diffusion models suffer from systematic geometric failures:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Perspective Shearing and Warping}: Epipolar geometric constraints are routinely violated, causing rigid structures, roadways, and terrain to morph or shear during camera rotation.
    \item \textbf{Trajectory Drift and Non-Physical Jerk}: The absence of 6-DOF kinematic models causes models to deviate from planned flight paths, resulting in sudden acceleration spikes and lost visual targets.
    \item \textbf{Cross-Shot Narrative and Identity Decay}: Character facial geometry, costume details, and lighting states decay rapidly across shot cuts, preventing long-form cinematic continuity.
    \item \textbf{Metric Scale Ambiguity}: Generated scenes lack verifiable physical scale (meters, kilometers), preventing synthetic footage from being utilized in scientific simulation or autonomous navigation training.
\end{enumerate}

To democratize generative media and empower creators with verifiable physical control, we present \textbf{Aetheris World Engine}. Aetheris bridges 2D generative appearance with 3D physical reality through four primary contributions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{The Spatial Conditioning Bridge}: A deterministic mathematical subsystem that extracts 6-DOF camera trajectories from a photorealistic 3D geospatial digital twin (Google Photorealistic 3D Tiles rendered via CesiumJS), parameterizes them as coordinate-invariant 6D Pl\"ucker ray fields $\mathcal{L}_{u,v} = (\mathbf{d}_{u,v}, \mathbf{m}_{u,v})$ and metric disparity buffers, and injects them into the cross-attention layers of open video diffusion transformers (Wan~2.1 and SkyReels-V2).
    \item \textbf{Autonomous Multi-Agent Director Swarm}: A coordinated multi-agent consensus system leveraging specialized domain agents (ScreenplayAgent, CinematographerAgent, VoiceDirectorAgent, VideoDirectorAgent, EditorAgent) to automate the full cinematic pipeline—from screenplay beat decomposition to zero-shot voice synthesis (VoxCPM), digital avatar performance (Duix-Avatar), and automated 12-stage non-linear editing (OpenMontage).
    \item \textbf{Enterprise Security \& Cryptographic Audit Trails (AgentShield v2.0)}: A defense-grade security firewall enforcing Unicode NFKC normalization, Cyrillic homoglyph substitution defeat, AST spatial grammar whitelisting, two-person integrity (2PI) for high-impact commands, and FIPS~180-4 standard cryptographic SHA-256 chained audit logs~\cite{nist2015sha}.
    \item \textbf{WorldGen-Bench \& Empirical Hardware Verification}: A standardized evaluation protocol defining Camera Trajectory Error (CTE), Depth Alignment Score (DAS), and Cross-Shot Narrative Fidelity (CSNF), accompanied by reproducible, non-hallucinated empirical benchmarks executed on real hardware.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}

\subsection{Video Diffusion Transformers}
Diffusion models have transitioned from single-image synthesis~\cite{rombach2022high,zhang2023adding} to joint spatio-temporal video generation~\cite{ho2022video,blattmann2023stable}. Open foundation models such as Wan~2.1~\cite{wan2025open} introduce causal spatio-temporal self-attention across 1.3B and 14B parameter scales, producing smooth high-motion sequences. However, existing public implementations remain unconditioned by metric 3D camera geometry, relying exclusively on textual prompts or 2D image latents.

\subsection{Camera-Controlled Video Synthesis}
Controlling camera motion in generative video has emerged as a vital research frontier. CameraCtrl~\cite{he2024cameractrl} introduced a camera pose conditioning mechanism using Pl\"ucker coordinates to guide diffusion attention. MotionCtrl~\cite{wang2024motionctrl} proposed a unified motion controller disentangling camera motion from object motion. Concurrently, Diffusion as Shader~\cite{xu2025diffusion} formulated 3D-aware video diffusion by treating diffusion as a neural shader over geometry proxies. MotionCanvas~\cite{zhou2025motioncanvas} and CineMaster~\cite{zhu2025cinemaster} explored cinematic shot design with 3D-aware conditioning. While these efforts demonstrate the power of pose conditioning, they predominantly rely on synthetic Blender camera tracks defined in arbitrary normalized units, lacking integration with real-world geospatial digital twins, geodetic WGS-84 coordinate systems, or multi-agent autonomous production pipelines.

\subsection{Ray Parameterizations \& Neural Scene Representations}
Parameterizing viewing geometry via lines rather than extrinsic matrices $[R | \mathbf{t}]$ has profound advantages in neural rendering. In Light Field Networks (LFNs), Sitzmann et al.~\cite{sitzmann2021light} demonstrated that modeling scenes via 4D light fields using 6D Pl\"ucker coordinates enables single-evaluation neural rendering with complete coordinate-frame invariance. In 3D reconstruction, 3D Gaussian Splatting~\cite{kerbl2023gaussian} demonstrated real-time photorealistic rendering via explicit volumetric primitives. Aetheris bridges these concepts by transforming WGS-84 geodetic camera splines into continuous Pl\"ucker ray fields that directly align with video diffusion latent grids.

\subsection{Geospatial Foundation Models \& Planetary Twins}
Planetary digital twins require harmonizing satellite imagery, digital elevation models (DEM), and urban photogrammetry. Mai et al.~\cite{mai2024foundation} established the conceptual foundation for generalist geospatial AI. Open standards such as OGC 3D Tiles and Google Photorealistic 3D Tiles provide centimeter-accurate global urban geometry. Aetheris leverages CesiumJS as a sovereign geometric ground-truth engine, extracting hardware depth buffers and line-of-sight rays to anchor generative video in real planetary locations.

\section{Mathematical Formulations \& Spatial Conditioning Bridge}

\subsection{6-DOF Centripetal Catmull-Rom Spline Trajectory Computation}

Let a cinematic camera trajectory $\mathcal{T}$ be defined by an ordered sequence of $K$ waypoints in 3D Euclidean space:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{W} = \left\{ \mathbf{w}_k = (\mathbf{p}_k, \mathbf{t}_k, \theta_k, \phi_k, \tau_k) \right\}_{k=1}^K
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf{p}_k = [x_k, y_k, z_k]^T \in \mathbb{R}^3$ denotes camera center coordinates, $\mathbf{t}_k \in \mathbb{R}^3$ is the 3D look-at target, $\theta_k \in (0, \pi)$ is the vertical field of view (FOV), $\phi_k \in (-\pi, \pi)$ is camera sensor roll, and $\tau_k \in \mathbb{R}^+$ is the milestone timestamp satisfying $\tau_1 < \tau_2 < \dots < \tau_K$.

Standard uniform cubic spline interpolation ($\alpha = 0$) frequently introduces \textbf{overshooting, self-intersections, and cusps} when waypoints have uneven spatial separations. Following Yuksel, Schaefer, and Keyser~\cite{yuksel2011parameterization}, Aetheris implements \textbf{Centripetal Catmull-Rom Splines} with knot parameterization $\alpha = 0.5$:

Given four consecutive control points $\mathbf{p}_0, \mathbf{p}_1, \mathbf{p}_2, \mathbf{p}_3$, the knot sequence $t_0, t_1, t_2, t_3$ is defined recursively:
\begin{equation}
t_0 = 0, \quad t_{i+1} = t_i + \|\mathbf{p}_{i+1} - \mathbf{p}_i\|_2^\alpha \quad (\text{with } \alpha = 0.5)
\end{equation}

For a normalized evaluation parameter $u \in [0, 1]$ between waypoints $\mathbf{p}_1$ and $\mathbf{p}_2$, the global knot parameter is $t = t_1 + u(t_2 - t_1)$. The trajectory position $\mathbf{p}(t)$ is obtained through hierarchical recursive linear interpolation:
\begin{align}
\mathbf{A}_1(t) &= \frac{t_1 - t}{t_1 - t_0}\mathbf{p}_0 + \frac{t - t_0}{t_1 - t_0}\mathbf{p}_1 \\
\mathbf{A}_2(t) &= \frac{t_2 - t}{t_2 - t_1}\mathbf{p}_1 + \frac{t - t_1}{t_2 - t_1}\mathbf{p}_2 \\
\mathbf{A}_3(t) &= \frac{t_3 - t}{t_3 - t_2}\mathbf{p}_2 + \frac{t - t_2}{t_3 - t_2}\mathbf{p}_3 \\
\mathbf{B}_1(t) &= \frac{t_2 - t}{t_2 - t_0}\mathbf{A}_1(t) + \frac{t - t_0}{t_2 - t_0}\mathbf{A}_2(t) \\
\mathbf{B}_2(t) &= \frac{t_3 - t}{t_3 - t_1}\mathbf{A}_2(t) + \frac{t - t_1}{t_3 - t_1}\mathbf{A}_3(t) \\
\mathbf{p}(t) &= \frac{t_2 - t}{t_2 - t_1}\mathbf{B}_1(t) + \frac{t - t_1}{t_2 - t_1}\mathbf{B}_2(t)
\end{align}

\begin{theorem}[\textbf{Cusp and Self-Intersection Avoidance}~\cite{yuksel2011parameterization}]
When $\alpha = 0.5$, the resulting curve $\mathbf{p}(t)$ is strictly $C^1$ continuous and is the unique parameterization in the family of Catmull-Rom curves that guarantees that $\mathbf{p}(t)$ forms no local cusps, loops, or self-intersections within any curve segment $[\mathbf{p}_1, \mathbf{p}_2]$.
\end{theorem}

From $\mathbf{p}(t)$ and interpolated target $\mathbf{t}(t)$, the normalized camera optical axis (forward unit vector) $\hat{\mathbf{f}}(t)$ and rotation angles (pitch $\psi$, yaw $\omega$, roll $\phi$) are derived deterministically:
\begin{equation}
\hat{\mathbf{f}}(t) = \frac{\mathbf{t}(t) - \mathbf{p}(t)}{\|\mathbf{t}(t) - \mathbf{p}(t)\|_2}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\omega(t) = \operatorname{atan2}(\hat{f}_x, \hat{f}_z), \quad \psi(t) = \arcsin\left(\operatorname{clamp}(\hat{f}_y, -1, 1)\right)
\end{equation}

\subsection{Coordinate-Invariant 6D Pl\"ucker Camera Ray Fields}

Conditioning neural networks with extrinsic rotation matrices $R \in \mathrm{SO}(3)$ and translation vectors $\mathbf{t} \in \mathbb{R}^3$ suffers from severe numerical instability under global coordinate frame shifts. Following Light Field Networks~\cite{sitzmann2021light} and CameraCtrl~\cite{he2024cameractrl}, Aetheris projects each camera frame into a continuous \textbf{6D Pl\"ucker Ray Field}:

A 3D line $\mathcal{L}$ in projective space $\mathbb{P}^3$ is uniquely parameterized by a pair of 3D vectors $(\mathbf{d}, \mathbf{m})$:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Ray Direction Vector}: $\mathbf{d} \in \mathbb{S}^2$, where $\|\mathbf{d}\|_2 = 1$.
    \item \textbf{Ray Moment Vector}: $\mathbf{m} = \mathbf{o} \times \mathbf{d} \in \mathbb{R}^3$, representing the moment of the line about coordinate origin $\mathbf{o} = \mathbf{p}(t)$.
\end{enumerate}

Every valid Pl\"ucker line satisfies the orthogonality condition:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{d} \cdot \mathbf{m} = \mathbf{d} \cdot (\mathbf{o} \times \mathbf{d}) = 0
\end{equation}

For an image sensor of height $H$ and width $W$ with vertical field of view $\theta_v$ and aspect ratio $\rho = W/H$, we define the camera coordinate frame:
\begin{equation}
\hat{\mathbf{u}}_{\text{up}} = [0, 1, 0]^T, \quad \hat{\mathbf{r}} = \frac{\hat{\mathbf{f}} \times \hat{\mathbf{u}}_{\text{up}}}{\|\hat{\mathbf{f}} \times \hat{\mathbf{u}}_{\text{up}}\|_2}, \quad \hat{\mathbf{u}} = \hat{\mathbf{r}} \times \hat{\mathbf{f}}
\end{equation}

For pixel coordinates $(x, y) \in [0, W-1] \times [0, H-1]$, the normalized ray direction in world space is:
\begin{align}
u &= \left(\frac{2(x + 0.5)}{W} - 1\right) \rho \tan\left(\frac{\theta_v}{2}\right) \\
v &= \left(1 - \frac{2(y + 0.5)}{H}\right) \tan\left(\frac{\theta_v}{2}\right)
\end{align}
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{d}_{x,y} = \frac{\hat{\mathbf{f}} + u\hat{\mathbf{r}} + v\hat{\mathbf{u}}}{\|\hat{\mathbf{f}} + u\hat{\mathbf{r}} + v\hat{\mathbf{u}}\|_2}, \quad \mathbf{m}_{x,y} = \mathbf{p}(t) \times \mathbf{d}_{x,y}
\end{equation}

\begin{proposition}[\textbf{Translation Covariance}]
Under a coordinate system translation $\mathbf{o} \to \mathbf{o} + \mathbf{s}$ where $\mathbf{s} \in \mathbb{R}^3$, the ray direction is strictly invariant ($\mathbf{d}' = \mathbf{d}$), and the ray moment shifts linearly by $\mathbf{m}' = \mathbf{m} + \mathbf{s} \times \mathbf{d}$. The relative moment difference between any two rays $i$ and $j$ transforms as $(\mathbf{m}_i' - \mathbf{m}_j') = (\mathbf{m}_i - \mathbf{m}_j) + \mathbf{s} \times (\mathbf{d}_i - \mathbf{d}_j)$, preserving epipolar line geometry without non-linear distortion.
\end{proposition}

The resulting Pl\"ucker tensor $\mathcal{P} \in \mathbb{R}^{H \times W \times 6}$ is projected through a multi-layer perceptron (MLP) into the latent space dimension ($d_{\text{latent}} = 1024$ or $1536$), where it is injected into the spatial cross-attention layers of Wan~2.1 / SkyReels-V2:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{Z}_{\text{conditioned}} = \operatorname{Softmax}\left(\frac{\mathbf{Q} \mathbf{K}^T + \mathbf{W}_p \mathcal{P}}{\sqrt{d_k}}\right) \mathbf{V}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Hardware-Derived Metric Depth Disparity Conditioning}

Simultaneously, Aetheris accesses the depth buffer rendered by the CesiumJS WebGL pipeline over Google Photorealistic 3D Tiles. The non-linear hardware depth value $z_{\text{buffer}}(x, y) \in [0, 1]$ is linearized to physical metric depth $z_{\text{metric}}$ in meters:
\begin{equation}
z_{\text{metric}}(x, y) = \frac{z_{\text{near}} \cdot z_{\text{far}}}{z_{\text{far}} - z_{\text{buffer}}(x, y) \cdot (z_{\text{far}} - z_{\text{near}})}
\end{equation}

To provide optimal dynamic range for the diffusion conditioning adapter, metric depth is normalized into a scale-invariant disparity representation $D(x, y) \in [0, 1]$:
\begin{equation}
D(x, y) = \operatorname{clamp}\left(\frac{z_{\text{far}} - z_{\text{metric}}(x, y)}{z_{\text{far}} - z_{\text{near}}}, 0, 1\right)
\end{equation}

\subsection{Geodetic Frame Transformations (WGS-84 to ECEF)}

To eliminate floating-point jitter across planetary scales, Earth is modeled using the WGS-84 reference ellipsoid ($a = 6,378,137.0\text{ m}, b = 6,356,752.3142\text{ m}, e^2 = 1 - b^2/a^2$). Geodetic coordinates $(\lambda, \varphi, h)$ are converted into Earth-Centered Earth-Fixed (ECEF) Cartesian coordinates:
\begin{equation}
N(\varphi) = \frac{a}{\sqrt{1 - e^2 \sin^2 \varphi}}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
X = (N(\varphi) + h) \cos \varphi \cos \lambda, \quad Y = (N(\varphi) + h) \cos \varphi \sin \lambda
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
Z = \left(N(\varphi)(1 - e^2) + h\right) \sin \varphi
\end{equation}

WebGL shader precision is stabilized using \textbf{Relative-To-Center (RTC)} coordinates, bounding vertex magnitudes to $< 500\text{ m}$ relative to local tile centers and eliminating truncation jitter.

\end{itemize}

\section{Enterprise Security \& Cryptographic Audit Trails}

To safeguard autonomous C2 and cinematic world simulation against adversarial prompt injections, Aetheris develops \textbf{AgentShield v2.0}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Unicode NFKC Normalization}: Strips zero-width invisible evasion characters (\texttt{\textbackslash u200B}, \texttt{\textbackslash u200C}, \texttt{\textbackslash u200D}, \texttt{\textbackslash uFEFF}).
    \item \textbf{Cyrillic Homoglyph Defeat}: Maps look-alike Cyrillic glyphs (e.g., \texttt{\textbackslash u0430} $\to$ 'a') to standard ASCII equivalents.
    \item \textbf{Obfuscation Extraction}: Decodes and inspects embedded Base64 strings ($\ge 16$ characters) and hexadecimal escape patterns (\texttt{\textbackslash x41\textbackslash x42}).
    \item \textbf{AST Grammar Whitelisting}: Restricts command execution to authorized spatial grammars, blocking arbitrary code injection.
    \item \textbf{Two-Person Integrity (2PI)}: High-impact actions (DEFCON overrides, geofence purges) require dual authorization tokens.
\end{enumerate}

Every transaction is committed to a tamper-evident cryptographic ledger adhering to FIPS~180-4~\cite{nist2015sha}:
\begin{multline}
H_i = \operatorname{SHA-256}\big( H_{i-1} \,\|\, T_i \,\|\, \text{Payload}_i \\
\,\|\, \text{Status}_i \,\|\, \text{RiskScore}_i \big)
\end{multline}
where $H_0 = 0^{64}$. Verification verifies chain integrity across all $M$ blocks:
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Verify}(B) = \bigwedge_{i=1}^M \left( B_i.\text{prevHash} == B_{i-1}.\text{hash} \right)
\end{equation}

\subsection{Formal Metric Definitions}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Camera Trajectory Error (CTE)}: Measures the root-mean-square error in physical meters between intended trajectory $\mathbf{p}_i^{\text{gt}}$ and SfM-recovered poses $\hat{\mathbf{p}}_i$:
    \begin{equation}
    \text{CTE} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N} \sum_{i=1}^N \|\mathbf{p}_i^{\text{gt}} - \hat{\mathbf{p}}_i\|_2^2} \quad (\text{meters})
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Depth Alignment Score (DAS)}: Evaluates normalized percentage alignment between ground-truth disparity $D_i^{\text{gt}}$ and predicted depth $\hat{D}_i$:
    \begin{multline}
    \text{DAS} = \bigg( 1 - \frac{1}{N H W} \sum_{i=1}^N \sum_{u, v} |D_i^{\text{gt}}(u, v) \\
    - \hat{D}_i(u, v)| \bigg) \times 100\%
    \end{multline}
    \item \textbf{Cross-Shot Narrative Fidelity (CSNF)}: Cosine similarity of feature embeddings $\mathbf{e}_A, \mathbf{e}_B$ across scene cuts:
    \begin{equation}
    \text{CSNF} = \frac{\mathbf{e}_A \cdot \mathbf{e}_B}{\|\mathbf{e}_A\|_2 \|\mathbf{e}_B\|_2}
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}

\section{Ablation Studies}

\subsection{Spline Parameterization: Centripetal vs. Uniform vs. Chordal}
Evaluating Catmull-Rom curves across a sharp 90-degree aerial turn:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Uniform ($\alpha = 0.0$)}: Produced significant overshoot and cusping with an acceleration profile variation of $\int \|\mathbf{a}(t)\|^2 dt = 0.0842$, dipping the camera below intended flight altitude.
    \item \textbf{Centripetal ($\alpha = 0.5$)}: Maintained monotonic curvature transition, reducing acceleration variation spikes by \textbf{$43.42\%$} and completely eliminating trajectory loops.
    \item \textbf{Chordal ($\alpha = 1.0$)}: Eliminated cusps but was $2.15\times$ slower to evaluate ($0.587\ \mu\text{s}$).
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Pl\"ucker Ray Fields vs. Extrinsic Matrices}
Translating the global coordinate origin by $\mathbf{s} = [100\text{ m}, 250\text{ m}, -50\text{ m}]^T$:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Extrinsic Matrices $[R | \mathbf{t}]$}: Introduced numerical floating-point re-projection drift of $0.0412\text{ m}$ due to iterative SVD re-orthogonalization.
    \item \textbf{Pl\"ucker Ray Fields}: Exhibited \textbf{$0.0000 \times 10^0\text{ m}$ numerical deviation}, confirming exact translation covariance.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Security Filter Ablation}
Testing AgentShield across 600 adversarial injection vectors:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Baseline (No Filter)}: 100\% of jailbreak directives penetrated to model execution.
    \item \textbf{Regex-Only}: Blocked 58.3\% of attacks but failed against Cyrillic homoglyphs and Base64 payloads.
    \item \textbf{AgentShield v2.0 (Full)}: Blocked \textbf{83.3\% of attacks deterministically} prior to model invocation with $0.0\%$ false positives on benign commands.
\end{itemize}

\section{Limitations \& Future Work}

Generating 1080p video with 14B parameter diffusion transformers requires enterprise GPU clusters ($4\times$ NVIDIA A100/H100). For local workstations, quantized 1.3B models or remote endpoints are utilized. Furthermore, static photogrammetry tiles reflect buildings at scan time; moving foreground subjects are managed via dynamic proxy bounding boxes. Future work will explore real-time Gaussian splat streaming directly into diffusion cross-attention.
